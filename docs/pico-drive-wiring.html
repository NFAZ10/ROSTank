Pico Drive Wiring

Raspberry Pi Pico to an L298N dual H-bridge and two encoder gear motors. Every pin here matches robot_ws/firmware/pico_drive/pico_drive.ino, so the firmware runs as-is once this is wired.

Encoders on 3.3 V onlyPico pins are not 5 V tolerant. Power the encoders from Pico pin 36, never from 5 V.
L298N +5V stays emptyThe Pico gets power from the Jetson's USB. Joining the two 5 V supplies can back-feed the Jetson.
Set the boost to 19 V firstAdjust the boost converter with a multimeter before the Jetson is plugged in. They often ship set to some other voltage.
Ground everything togetherBattery −, L298N GND and Pico GND must connect, or the motor signals have no reference.

Motor driver and power

Speed (PWM) Direction Motor power Battery + Ground

Pull the ENA and ENB jumpers off the L298N so the Pico can set speed. Leave the 5V-EN regulator jumper on while the battery is 12 V or less. Above 12 V, take it off and the L298N's logic needs a separate 5 V. The Jetson runs from the same battery on its own fused branch through a boost converter, never from the L298N's terminals (see System power).

System power

Battery + / 19 V Ground USB

The boost converter holds the Jetson at a steady 19 V while the pack drains from 12.6 V towards 9 V and while motor starts and stalls drag it down, so the Jetson doesn't brown out and reboot mid-drive. Feed it straight from the battery on its own 5 A fuse rather than from the L298N's terminals. The Jetson draws up to about 25 W, plus USB devices, which near an empty pack is about 3.5 A on the 12 V side. Use wire rated for at least 5 A on both branches.

Encoders

Encoder A Encoder B 3.3 V Ground

Encoder gear motors (JGB37-520 style) have a 6-wire lead. M+ and M− go to the L298N outputs above. The other four go here. Wire colors vary by seller, so go by the labels printed on the encoder board, not the colors. Run encoder wires away from the motor wires, or twist each motor pair, to keep switching noise out of the counts.

Physical pin numbers count with the USB port at the top: pins 1 to 20 run down the left edge, and 21 to 40 run back up the right edge. Pins 18 to 23 sit together at the bottom corner, which keeps the encoder wiring short.

Before first power-on

  1. Check there is continuity between battery −, the L298N GND terminal, the boost converter IN− and Pico pin 13, and none between battery + and ground.
  2. With the Jetson unplugged, switch on and set the boost converter output to 19.0 V on a multimeter. Switch off, then plug in the Jetson.
  3. Lift the robot so both tracks are off the ground.
  4. Plug the Pico into the Jetson, then switch on. The main switch powers the Jetson and the motors together. The motors stay still until the Jetson sends drive commands.
  5. Ask Claude to run the direction test. Each track runs slowly forward, then backward, and any reversed side gets flipped in the firmware.

Turning the encoders on

  • The encoders do nothing until HAS_ENCODERS is set to 1 in the firmware. Until then the drive runs open-loop.
  • The firmware counts both edges of channel A, so counts per sprocket revolution are:
TICKS_PER_REV = encoder PPR × 2 × gear ratio
e.g. 11 PPR × 2 × 30 = 660
  • Take the PPR and gear ratio from the motor's listing. If the track sprocket is geared off the motor shaft, multiply by that ratio too.
  • If a track's counts go down while it drives forward, swap that encoder's A and B wires.
